12 ◆ Procedure for Obtaining Patent

A patent is obtained through a systematic legal procedure involving filing, publication, examination and grant.

Patent Registration Process

Step Activity
1 Patent Search
2 Drafting Application
3 Filing Application
4 Publication
5 Request for Examination
6 Examination
7 Opposition (if any)
8 Grant of Patent
Patent protection begins only after the patent is granted.

13 ◆ Who Can Apply for a Patent?

  • True and First Inventor
  • Assignee of Inventor
  • Legal Representative of Inventor
  • Joint Inventors

The applicant must have a legal right over the invention before filing the patent application.

14 ◆ Patent Application

Patent Application is the formal request submitted to the Patent Office seeking protection for an invention.

Types of Applications

Type Description
Provisional Application Filed before complete invention development
Complete Application Contains full invention details
Convention Application Claims priority from foreign filing
PCT Application International patent application

15 ◆ Patent Specifications

Patent Specification describes the invention completely and clearly so that a skilled person can reproduce it.

Components of Specification

  • Title of Invention
  • Field of Invention
  • Background
  • Summary
  • Detailed Description
  • Drawings
  • Claims
  • Abstract
A complete specification is the most important document in a patent application.

16 ◆ Patent Claims

Claims define the legal scope of protection sought by the inventor.

The claims determine the boundaries of the patent right and identify what is legally protected.

Importance of Claims

  • Determine legal protection.
  • Prevent unauthorized use.
  • Define patent boundaries.
  • Help resolve infringement disputes.

17 ◆ Publication of Patent Application

Publication makes the patent application available to the public.

Normally, publication occurs after 18 months from the filing date unless early publication is requested.

Benefits of Publication

  • Public disclosure of invention.
  • Transparency in patent system.
  • Creates prior art.
  • Allows public inspection.

18 ◆ Examination of Patent Application

Examination is the process by which the Patent Office verifies whether the invention satisfies patentability requirements.

Examiner Checks

  • Novelty
  • Inventive Step
  • Industrial Applicability
  • Legal Compliance
  • Sufficiency of Disclosure

First Examination Report (FER)

The examiner issues a report highlighting objections and deficiencies that must be addressed by the applicant.

19 ◆ Opposition to Grant of Patent

Opposition allows third parties to challenge the patent application before or after grant.

Grounds for Opposition

  • Lack of Novelty
  • Obviousness
  • Wrongful Obtaining
  • Insufficient Disclosure
  • Prior Publication
  • Prior Use
Type When Filed
Pre-Grant Opposition Before Patent Grant
Post-Grant Opposition After Patent Grant

20 ◆ Grant and Sealing of Patent

After successful examination and resolution of objections, the Patent Office grants the patent.

The patentee receives exclusive rights to make, use, sell and license the invention.

Benefits of Patent Grant

  • Exclusive commercial rights.
  • Licensing opportunities.
  • Technology transfer.
  • Market advantage.
  • Legal protection.
Patent term in India is generally 20 years from the filing date.
