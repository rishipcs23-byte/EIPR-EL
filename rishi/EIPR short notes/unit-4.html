1 ◆ Introduction to Intellectual Property

Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the human intellect such as inventions, literary works, artistic works, symbols, names, designs and images used in commerce.

Intellectual property is an intangible asset that can provide commercial value to its owner. Protection of intellectual property encourages innovation, creativity and economic growth.

  • Protects innovation.
  • Encourages creativity.
  • Provides exclusive rights.
  • Promotes economic development.
IP transforms ideas and creativity into valuable assets.

2 ◆ Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

Intellectual Property Rights are legal rights granted to creators and inventors for protecting their intellectual creations.

IPR provides exclusive rights to use, manufacture, sell or license intellectual creations for a specified period.

  • Exclusive ownership rights.
  • Protection against infringement.
  • Encouragement for innovation.
  • Commercial exploitation opportunities.

3 ◆ Types of Intellectual Property Rights

Type Purpose
Copyright Protects literary and artistic works
Trademark Protects brand identity
Patent Protects inventions
Industrial Design Protects appearance of products
Trade Secret Protects confidential business information

4 ◆ Copyright

Copyright is a legal right granted to creators of literary, artistic, musical and dramatic works.

Copyright protects original expressions of ideas but not the ideas themselves.

  • Books
  • Software
  • Music
  • Films
  • Artistic Works

5 ◆ Trademark

A trademark is a sign, symbol, word, logo or combination used to distinguish products or services of one enterprise from others.
  • Brand recognition.
  • Consumer trust.
  • Legal protection.
  • Business goodwill.

6 ◆ Patent

A patent is an exclusive right granted for an invention that provides a new technical solution or process.

Patent protection generally lasts for 20 years from the filing date.

  • Exclusive rights.
  • Commercial advantage.
  • Technology disclosure.
  • Licensing opportunities.

7 ◆ Industrial Design Rights

Industrial Design Rights protect the aesthetic appearance of products rather than their functional aspects.
  • Shape
  • Configuration
  • Pattern
  • Ornamentation
  • Visual appearance

8 ◆ Trade Secrets

Trade Secrets are confidential business information that provide a competitive advantage.
  • Formulas
  • Manufacturing processes
  • Customer lists
  • Business strategies
Example: Coca-Cola formula is protected as a trade secret.

9 ◆ Salient Features of Patent

Requirement Description
Novelty Must be new
Inventive Step Must not be obvious
Industrial Application Must be useful in industry

Novelty

The invention should not have been disclosed anywhere in the world before filing.

Inventive Step

The invention should involve technical advancement and should not be obvious to skilled persons.

Industrial Application

The invention should be capable of practical industrial use.

10 ◆ Patentable Inventions

  • New products.
  • New processes.
  • Technical innovations.
  • Industrial applications.
  • Novel engineering solutions.

11 ◆ Non-Patentable Inventions

  • Scientific discoveries.
  • Mathematical methods.
  • Business methods.
  • Traditional knowledge.
  • Agricultural methods.
  • Atomic energy inventions.
Not every invention is patentable under Indian Patent Law.

12 ◆ Procedure for Obtaining Patent

A patent is obtained through a systematic legal procedure involving filing, publication, examination and grant.

Patent Registration Process

Step Activity
1 Patent Search
2 Drafting Application
3 Filing Application
4 Publication
5 Request for Examination
6 Examination
7 Opposition (if any)
8 Grant of Patent
Patent protection begins only after the patent is granted.

13 ◆ Who Can Apply for a Patent?

  • True and First Inventor
  • Assignee of Inventor
  • Legal Representative of Inventor
  • Joint Inventors

The applicant must have a legal right over the invention before filing the patent application.

14 ◆ Patent Application

Patent Application is the formal request submitted to the Patent Office seeking protection for an invention.

Types of Applications

Type Description
Provisional Application Filed before complete invention development
Complete Application Contains full invention details
Convention Application Claims priority from foreign filing
PCT Application International patent application

15 ◆ Patent Specifications

Patent Specification describes the invention completely and clearly so that a skilled person can reproduce it.

Components of Specification

  • Title of Invention
  • Field of Invention
  • Background
  • Summary
  • Detailed Description
  • Drawings
  • Claims
  • Abstract
A complete specification is the most important document in a patent application.

16 ◆ Patent Claims

Claims define the legal scope of protection sought by the inventor.

The claims determine the boundaries of the patent right and identify what is legally protected.

Importance of Claims

  • Determine legal protection.
  • Prevent unauthorized use.
  • Define patent boundaries.
  • Help resolve infringement disputes.

17 ◆ Publication of Patent Application

Publication makes the patent application available to the public.

Normally, publication occurs after 18 months from the filing date unless early publication is requested.

Benefits of Publication

  • Public disclosure of invention.
  • Transparency in patent system.
  • Creates prior art.
  • Allows public inspection.

18 ◆ Examination of Patent Application

Examination is the process by which the Patent Office verifies whether the invention satisfies patentability requirements.

Examiner Checks

  • Novelty
  • Inventive Step
  • Industrial Applicability
  • Legal Compliance
  • Sufficiency of Disclosure

First Examination Report (FER)

The examiner issues a report highlighting objections and deficiencies that must be addressed by the applicant.

19 ◆ Opposition to Grant of Patent

Opposition allows third parties to challenge the patent application before or after grant.

Grounds for Opposition

  • Lack of Novelty
  • Obviousness
  • Wrongful Obtaining
  • Insufficient Disclosure
  • Prior Publication
  • Prior Use
Type When Filed
Pre-Grant Opposition Before Patent Grant
Post-Grant Opposition After Patent Grant

20 ◆ Grant and Sealing of Patent

After successful examination and resolution of objections, the Patent Office grants the patent.

The patentee receives exclusive rights to make, use, sell and license the invention.

Benefits of Patent Grant

  • Exclusive commercial rights.
  • Licensing opportunities.
  • Technology transfer.
  • Market advantage.
  • Legal protection.
Patent term in India is generally 20 years from the filing date.

21 ◆ Rights of Patentee

A patentee is the person whose name appears in the patent register as the owner of the patent.

Once a patent is granted, the patentee receives exclusive legal rights over the invention for a specified period.

Rights of Patentee

  • Right to manufacture the invention.
  • Right to use the invention.
  • Right to sell the invention.
  • Right to license the invention.
  • Right to assign ownership.
  • Right to take legal action against infringement.
Patent rights are territorial and valid only in the country where protection is granted.

22 ◆ Joint Inventors and Co-Owners

When two or more persons contribute to an invention, they may become joint inventors or co-owners of the patent.

Features

  • Equal ownership rights unless otherwise agreed.
  • Each owner may use the invention.
  • Revenue sharing may be contractually decided.
  • Disputes are resolved through legal agreements.

23 ◆ Limitations on Patent Rights

Patent rights are not absolute and may be restricted in public interest.

Limitations

  • Research and experimental use.
  • Government use.
  • Compulsory licensing.
  • National emergency provisions.
  • Public health requirements.
Patent law balances inventor rights and public welfare.

24 ◆ Government Use of Patented Inventions

The Government may use a patented invention for public purposes under specific legal provisions.

Government use is permitted for defense, healthcare, public welfare and national emergencies.

  • National security.
  • Public health.
  • Emergency situations.
  • Strategic industries.

25 ◆ Compulsory Licensing

Compulsory Licensing permits a third party to use a patented invention without the consent of the patent owner under special circumstances.

Reasons for Compulsory License

  • Public requirements not satisfied.
  • Excessive pricing.
  • Public health concerns.
  • Failure to work patent in India.
Condition Result
Patent not available to public Compulsory License possible
Unreasonable pricing Compulsory License possible
Public health emergency License may be granted

26 ◆ Defence Related Inventions

Certain inventions affecting national defense and security receive special treatment under patent law.
  • Restricted publication.
  • Confidential examination.
  • Government control.
  • Security considerations.
National security takes priority over public disclosure.

27 ◆ Revocation of Patent

Revocation means cancellation of a patent due to legal or technical defects.

Grounds for Revocation

  • Lack of novelty.
  • Obvious invention.
  • Wrongful obtaining.
  • Insufficient disclosure.
  • Fraudulent information.
Ground Explanation
Novelty Failure Invention already known
Obviousness No inventive step
Fraud False information submitted

28 ◆ Transfer of Patent Rights

Patent rights can be transferred from one person to another through legal mechanisms.

Transfer enables commercialization and broader utilization of patented technology.

Methods

  • Assignment
  • Licensing
  • Inheritance
  • Business acquisition

29 ◆ Assignment of Patent

Assignment is the transfer of ownership rights from the patentee to another person.

Types of Assignment

  • Complete Assignment
  • Partial Assignment
  • Conditional Assignment

The assignee becomes the new patent owner after assignment.

30 ◆ Licensing of Patent

Licensing allows another party to use a patent while ownership remains with the patentee.
Type Description
Exclusive License Only one licensee allowed
Non-Exclusive License Multiple licensees permitted
Sole License Single licensee plus owner use
Licensing generates revenue while retaining ownership.

31 ◆ Register of Patents

The Register of Patents is the official record maintained by the Patent Office.

Contains Information About

  • Patent Number
  • Patentee Details
  • Assignment Records
  • Licenses
  • Amendments
  • Expiry Information

The register serves as legal evidence of patent ownership and status.

32 ◆ Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms including plants, animals, microorganisms and ecosystems present on Earth.

Biodiversity plays a vital role in maintaining ecological balance and provides valuable biological resources for food, medicine, agriculture and industry.

Levels of Biodiversity

  • Genetic Diversity
  • Species Diversity
  • Ecosystem Diversity
India is one of the world's mega-diverse countries with rich biological resources.

33 ◆ Intellectual Property Rights and Biodiversity

IPR and biodiversity are closely related because biological resources are often used in inventions, medicines and agricultural innovations.

Patent protection should not encourage exploitation of biological resources without proper authorization and benefit sharing.

Objectives

  • Conservation of biodiversity.
  • Sustainable utilization.
  • Fair benefit sharing.
  • Protection against biopiracy.

34 ◆ Traditional Knowledge

Traditional Knowledge (TK) refers to knowledge, innovations and practices developed by indigenous and local communities over generations.

Traditional knowledge often relates to medicine, agriculture, biodiversity conservation and cultural practices.

Characteristics

  • Community owned.
  • Passed through generations.
  • Not usually documented.
  • Closely linked with biodiversity.
Examples include Ayurvedic medicine, Yoga and traditional farming techniques.

35 ◆ Biopiracy

Biopiracy refers to unauthorized use or commercialization of biological resources and traditional knowledge without compensation to indigenous communities.

Examples

  • Neem Patent Controversy
  • Turmeric Patent Case
  • Basmati Rice Patent Dispute

Biopiracy raises ethical and legal concerns regarding ownership and benefit sharing.

36 ◆ Trade Secrets

Trade Secrets are confidential business information that provide economic value because they are not publicly known.

Unlike patents, trade secrets remain protected as long as secrecy is maintained.

Examples

  • Coca-Cola Formula
  • Google Search Algorithm
  • Manufacturing Processes
  • Customer Databases

37 ◆ Characteristics of Trade Secrets

  • Confidential in nature.
  • Commercial value exists.
  • Provides competitive advantage.
  • Protected through secrecy.
  • No registration required.

38 ◆ Trade Secret vs Patent

Trade Secret Patent
No registration required Registration required
Unlimited protection period 20-year protection
Maintained through secrecy Public disclosure required
Lower cost Higher filing cost
Protection lost if disclosed Protected after disclosure
Trade Secret = Secrecy Based Protection Patent = Legal Registration Based Protection

39 ◆ Protection of Trade Secrets

Organizations use contractual, technical and legal measures to protect trade secrets.

Protection Methods

  • Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs)
  • Confidentiality Clauses
  • Restricted Access
  • Password Protection
  • Employee Training
  • Information Security Policies

40 ◆ Bayer Corporation vs Union of India

This landmark case dealt with pharmaceutical patents and patent linkage issues in India.

Key Points

  • Bayer sought restrictions on generic drug approvals.
  • Delhi High Court rejected patent linkage concept.
  • Patent rights and drug approval processes were treated separately.
The case strengthened the balance between patent protection and public access to medicines.

41 ◆ RiceTec Basmati Patent Case

RiceTec, a US company, obtained patents relating to Basmati rice varieties and characteristics.

Issue

  • India challenged the patent claims.
  • Concerns arose regarding traditional knowledge and geographical identity.
  • Several patent claims were withdrawn.

Importance

  • Highlighted biopiracy concerns.
  • Strengthened protection of traditional knowledge.
  • Demonstrated importance of biodiversity protection.
