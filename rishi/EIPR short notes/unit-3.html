1 ◆ Entrepreneurial Marketing and Sales

Entrepreneurial Marketing and Sales combines traditional marketing and sales principles with agility, creativity and risk-taking commonly seen in entrepreneurial ventures.

This approach is especially important for startups and small businesses where resources are limited but growth requirements are high. Entrepreneurs rely on innovative and cost-effective marketing strategies to attract customers and increase market share.

Entrepreneurial Marketing

  • Focuses on customer-centric strategies.
  • Uses social media and word-of-mouth promotion.
  • Employs low-cost marketing methods.
  • Builds strong brand identity.
  • Adapts quickly to market changes.

Entrepreneurial Sales

  • Founder-led customer interaction.
  • Direct customer feedback collection.
  • Relationship-based selling.
  • Continuous sales improvement.
Entrepreneurial marketing emphasizes innovation, customer understanding, experimentation and growth orientation.

2 ◆ Marketing Mix (4Ps)

Marketing Mix refers to a set of controllable marketing tools used by businesses to influence customers and achieve organizational objectives.

The four elements of the marketing mix are Product, Price, Place and Promotion. These elements must work together to create a successful marketing strategy.

Element Description
Product Goods or services offered to customers
Price Amount paid by customers
Place Distribution and availability
Promotion Communication with customers
All 4Ps must be aligned to deliver a consistent customer experience.

3 ◆ Product

A product is anything offered to satisfy customer needs and wants. It may be tangible or intangible.
  • Physical Goods
  • Services
  • Software
  • Digital Products
  • Experiences

The success of a product depends on its quality, design, functionality, packaging and ability to solve customer problems.

4 ◆ Product Life Cycle (PLC)

Product Life Cycle describes the stages through which a product passes from introduction to decline.
Stage Characteristics
Introduction New product launch
Growth Rapid increase in sales
Maturity Peak market penetration
Decline Reduction in sales

Introduction Stage

  • Product newly launched.
  • High marketing expenses.
  • Low sales volume.
  • Focus on awareness creation.

Growth Stage

  • Increasing sales.
  • Higher profitability.
  • Expansion of distribution channels.
  • Competitors enter the market.

Maturity Stage

  • Sales growth slows.
  • Market saturation occurs.
  • Customer retention becomes important.
  • Heavy competition.

Decline Stage

  • Falling demand.
  • Technological replacement.
  • Reduced profitability.
  • Product phase-out planning.

5 ◆ Price

Price is the amount of money customers pay to obtain a product or service.
  • Determines profitability.
  • Influences demand.
  • Affects brand perception.
  • Impacts competitive position.

6 ◆ Pricing Strategies

Strategy Description
Cost Plus Pricing Cost + Profit Margin
Value Based Pricing Based on customer perception
Competitive Pricing Based on competitors
Psychological Pricing Influences customer psychology
Dynamic Pricing Changes according to demand

7 ◆ Place (Distribution)

Place refers to activities involved in making products available to customers.
  • Product availability
  • Inventory management
  • Transportation
  • Warehousing
  • Customer accessibility

8 ◆ Distribution Channels

Channel Description
Direct Producer → Consumer
Indirect Uses intermediaries
Hybrid Combination approach

9 ◆ Promotion

Promotion includes all communication activities used to inform, persuade and remind customers about products.
  • Advertising
  • Sales Promotion
  • Public Relations
  • Personal Selling
  • Direct Marketing
  • Digital Marketing
